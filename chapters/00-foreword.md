## Notes for the draft

The Foreword is the author's voice unfiltered. It establishes credibility and stakes simultaneously. Topics to weave in:

### Personal arc
- Burroughs B-1900 at Cook Children's Hospital, Fort Worth. Age 16, night shift.
- TCU — Technology and Philosophy.
- PC Company (1986).
- CGL — the 4GL generating C/Unix applications.
- Open Systems Group, founded on CGL. Inc. 500 #127 in 1996.
- Para-Docs Software.
- The present moment with Webspinner. Webspinner as the second major act of invention, not retirement.

### The PC democratization, lived
- Not as history, as memory. The author was *there* — at the inflection point when computing power left the glass-walled data center and arrived on the desks of ordinary people.
- The pattern that broke: priesthood of the mainframe operators, the IBM monopoly, the assumption that real computing required real institutions.
- What changed it: cheap commodity hardware, open standards, hobbyist and small-business demand, and a generation of builders who refused to accept the priesthood's framing.

### Why this book exists
- The same pattern is repeating with Synthetic Intelligence. Different priesthood, same structure.
- Someone who saw it the first time has a duty to point it out.
- Not as nostalgia. As warning, and as instruction.

### The moral imperative
- This is not a business book.
- It is not a technical manual, though it contains technical substance.
- It is a manifesto from someone who has built before, is building now, and is asking the reader to build alongside.

### The Webspinner Foundation
- What it is: a movement that happens to ship software.
- What it stands for: sovereign, moral, sustainable Synthetic Intelligence.
- What it asks of the reader: to take the question of who owns the synthetic mind as seriously as the previous generation took the question of who owned the personal computer.

### Closing
- The book that follows makes a technical case, an economic case, an environmental case, and a moral case. They reinforce each other.
- The reader does not need to be a technologist to understand what is at stake.
- The reader does need to act.
