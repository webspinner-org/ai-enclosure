# ILLUSTRATIONS.md

Image generation prompts for *AI Enclosure*. Designed for OpenAI's image models (gpt-image-1 / DALL·E 3), but the prompts are written naturally enough to work with most text-to-image systems. Where a prompt is technical (diagrams with precise labels), a structured-spec alternative is provided so the artwork can be rendered in a vector tool such as Excalidraw, Figma, Draw.io, or OmniGraffle, where text reliably stays legible.

---

## Visual Identity (read first; every chapter prompt builds on this)

The book argues for a deliberate, considered, historically-grounded alternative to the gloss-and-neon aesthetic of contemporary AI marketing. The visual identity should reflect the argument.

**Recommended unified style:**

> *A pen-and-ink illustration with restrained selective watercolor wash. Composition and line quality should evoke late-19th-century technical engraving, early-20th-century scientific illustration (think Audubon, Diderot's Encyclopédie, William Morris pattern work, the Whole Earth Catalog's drawn-not-photographed sensibility). Color palette is muted and earthy — warm parchment ivory, indigo, ochre, rust, deep forest green, charcoal black. Gold or copper accents used sparingly for emphasis. No neon, no chrome, no glossy gradients, no cyber-purple, no glowing brains. The goal is to look like an artifact one would want to keep, not a tech-blog header one would scroll past.*

**Recurring visual motifs that should thread through the whole book:**

- The **loom** (warp threads stretched vertical, weft passing horizontally, shuttle in motion) — the book's central metaphor.
- The **Cell** as a small structure or building rather than a digital abstraction.
- **Hands at work** — human hands operating tools, never disembodied glowing figures.
- **Light from inside small dwellings** — for the cooperative-federation imagery.
- **Industrial-scale apparatus** rendered with the slight menace of 19th-century factory engravings — for the hyperscale chapters.
- **No human faces in close-up.** Figures appear at middle distance, often from behind. The book is not about specific personalities.

**Aspect ratios:**
- Cover: 2:3 portrait (1024×1536 or equivalent)
- Chapter openers: 3:2 landscape (1536×1024) or 1:1 square — pick one and stay consistent
- Reference Architecture diagram: 16:9 landscape, oversized (3072×1728 or larger)

**Negative prompts to include in every generation:**
- *no neon, no chrome gradients, no glowing blue circuit board, no cyber-purple, no abstract floating brain, no Silicon Valley business-stock imagery, no AI-art uncanny faces, no text on signs unless explicitly described*

## Cover

**Title and subtitle to overlay (do not generate text in the image; typeset separately):**
- *AI Enclosure*
- *Why Sovereign Intelligence Demands Warp Speed*
- *John D. Marx*

**Prompt:**

> A pen-and-ink illustration with selective watercolor wash, in the style of a late-19th-century book frontispiece. Center composition: a wooden weaving loom of considerable size, mid-work, with vertical warp threads of indigo and ochre stretched under tension. A wooden shuttle is suspended mid-flight halfway across the loom. Behind the loom, in the deep background and rendered smaller and slightly faded, is a vast industrial-scale data center — concrete monoliths, cooling stacks venting steam, a chain-link fence with a padlock cutting across the foreground at the base of the data center. The fence is being either crossed or unmade by the threads of the loom in front of it; the foreground loom is the answer to the background fence. Warm parchment ivory background paper. Earthy palette — indigo, ochre, deep rust, charcoal. A single thin line of copper-gold runs along one warp thread, catching light. Composition is portrait orientation, 2:3 ratio. No text in the image. The mood is serious, considered, hopeful — a manifesto cover, not a product cover.

**Alternative cover concept (simpler, more iconic):**

> A single hand, viewed from behind and slightly above, holding open a book whose pages are made of woven cloth — the warp threads visible, individual letters legible only as tiny stitched marks. The hand is calloused, working-class, ungloved. Out of the open book rise threads of indigo and ochre that climb up off the page and weave themselves into a soft loom-frame visible above. Behind the figure, faint, the silhouette of a vast data center pierced by the loom's threads. Pen and ink with watercolor wash. Earthy palette. Portrait, 2:3. The book within the cover is the book the reader is holding.

## Front Matter

### A Note on Authorship

> A wooden writing desk seen from above and slightly behind. On the desk: an open notebook with handwritten lines, a fountain pen, a pair of reading glasses, and — overlaid above the notebook in delicate pen-and-ink — a faint hovering wireframe of a digital terminal with a single em-dash visible mid-line, as if the desk and the terminal are coexisting in the same plane. Hands of an older man, calloused, at rest near the notebook, not actively writing. Warm parchment background. Ochre and charcoal palette with one thin copper-gold em-dash running across the page. Composition emphasizes that the human and the synthetic instrument are working together, and that the human is in the foreground.

### Foreword

> A composite hand-drawn collage in the style of an old printer's keepsake page. Five small panels arranged in a soft S-curve down the page, each a vignette: (1) a teenage night-shift operator standing beside a Burroughs B-1900 mainframe at age 20 (figure from behind only), the early 1980s feel of fluorescent-lit machine room; (2) a TCU graduation cap and a wedding ring overlapping on an open philosophy book; (3) the Inc. 500 medallion lying on a desk with a 1990s PC keyboard; (4) a small chapel doorway with a rolled academic sash; (5) a baby's hand resting on the open page of a Tao Te Ching. All five panels rendered as engravings with selective watercolor. The vignettes are connected by a single thread of copper-gold that runs through them, ending in a present-day Apple Silicon laptop at the base of the composition. The thread is a life. Earthy palette, parchment background. No close-up faces.

### A Note on Terminology

> Two parchment scrolls laid side by side on a wooden table, partly unrolled. The left scroll bears the word "ARTIFICIAL" in fading antique calligraphy, the letters slightly worn. The right scroll bears the word "SYNTHETIC" in fresh, deliberate calligraphy, the ink still glossy. A pen rests across both. Above the scrolls, faintly, the words "SOVEREIGN INTELLIGENCE" appear in a different hand, as if newly inscribed. Warm light from the upper left. Pen and ink with watercolor wash. Composition is square or 4:3. The image argues, by composition, that words are chosen deliberately and have consequences.

## Part I — The Why

### Chapter 1 — The Hyperscale Trap

> A vast data center rendered as a 19th-century industrial engraving. Concrete monoliths stretch back into a hazed distance. Massive cooling towers vent plumes of white steam. In the foreground, a single residential house sits beside a power substation, its windows lit warmly — but a high-voltage transmission line passes directly over the house, sagging slightly under load. Below the data center, a small river runs almost dry. The mood is the menace of unchecked industrial expansion seen through the eye of a sympathetic but alarmed observer. Pen and ink with rust and ochre wash, charcoal shadows. Landscape orientation. Echoes of Lewis Hine's industrial photography and Doré's London engravings.

### Chapter 2 — The Concentration Problem

> A printer's pressroom rendered in pen-and-ink engraving style. A single very large printing press dominates the room, its iron arms in motion. Around the press, almost invisibly small at the room's edges, a dozen smaller idle presses sit covered with cloth. From the dominant press, freshly printed broadsheets stream into the hands of a single figure (seen from behind) who is dressed in late-19th-century industrialist clothing. The smaller presses' covers are dust-laden. The mood is the moment Liebling's parenthetical described — an industry consolidating into a small number of hands. Earthy palette, parchment paper feel, charcoal shadowing. Landscape.

### Chapter 3 — The Privacy Collapse

> A wooden writing desk in a study. On the desk: an open journal, written in by a hand we cannot see. The journal page, however, is *transparent* — through it we see, faintly, that the same words are simultaneously appearing on the screen of an enormous building visible through the study's window in the distance, where countless similar journals are projected across its facade. A single pane of the study's window is cracked. The composition argues, by visual logic, that what was meant to be private has been replicated elsewhere without the writer's awareness. Pen-and-ink, watercolor wash, indigo and charcoal palette. Landscape.

### Chapter 4 — The Lessons of Computing History

> A large engraving in two panels, side by side, joined by a soft horizon line. Left panel: a glass-walled mainframe room of the 1970s, with white-coated operators working around a refrigerator-sized computer; a queue of ordinary people (nurses, accountants, teachers, seen from behind only) waits in a corridor outside. Right panel: a 1975 garage, doors open, with a kit-built personal computer on a workbench, hand-drawn schematics tacked to the wall, two figures (Wozniak and Felsenstein archetypes, viewed from behind, no faces) leaning over the bench with soldering irons. Between the two panels: a thread of copper-gold connecting them, running from the priesthood of the left to the hobbyists of the right. Pen-and-ink with selective wash, earthy palette. Landscape, two-panel.

## Part II — The What

### Chapter 5 — Defining Warp

> An exploded diagram of a wooden weaving loom, drawn in the style of Diderot's Encyclopédie plates. Each major component of the loom — frame, warp beam, heddles, shuttle, beater bar — is shown in its exploded position with thin numbered call-out lines, but the call-outs are unlabeled (the chapter labels them in prose). Wood-grain rendering, ink-line shading, parchment background. Composition is square or slightly landscape. The image works as both a beautiful technical illustration and as the visual key to the book's central metaphor.

### Chapter 6 — Green SI

> A residential desk seen from above and slightly behind. On the desk: an Apple Silicon laptop, open, its screen glowing softly. Around the laptop on the desk: a small potted plant, a coffee mug, a notebook. Through the room's window, a quiet residential street at evening, with windows of nearby houses lit warmly. A single power cord connects the laptop to a household outlet. The composition is intimate, ordinary, domestic. In strong contrast to the Chapter 1 industrial-scale image. Pen-and-ink with warm watercolor wash — golden-hour palette, soft indigo shadows. Landscape or square.

### Chapter 7 — Confidential SI

> A small wooden cabinet of curiosities, doors closed, sitting on a sturdy table. The cabinet has a single brass keyhole. A copper key on a leather thong rests on the table beside the cabinet, conspicuously in the user's possession. Around the cabinet, faintly, are radiating concentric lines suggesting that what is inside stays inside. Pen-and-ink with copper-gold key accents, deep indigo cabinet wood, parchment background. Square composition. The image's argument: the data is in your custody, the key is in your hand.

### Chapter 8 — Sovereign SI

> A foundation stone of pale limestone, partially set into the ground, with five short Latin-style inscriptions chiseled into its visible face — too small in the image to read individually, but their presence is clear. The five marks correspond to the five rights (own, inspect, modify, refuse, disconnect). Above the stone, the rough beginnings of a wall being built upward — the wall is the rest of the architecture. A single hand at lower right (calloused, working-class) rests on the stone, claiming it. Pen-and-ink with watercolor wash. Charcoal and indigo and parchment. Square or landscape.

### Chapter 9 — Moral AI

> A pair of antique brass scales of justice, set on a wooden table. In the left pan: a small folded paper with the word "POWER" written on it. In the right pan: an equally small folded paper, blank — representing the citizen's authority, written to be filled in by the person who holds it. The scales are perfectly level. Above and behind, faintly, the open page of a book — the page the reader is holding. Pen-and-ink with watercolor wash. Brass tones, indigo, charcoal. Square. The image's argument: moral weight is in balance only when the citizen holds something equal to the power being weighed.

### Chapter 10 — The Value Triangle

> A single equilateral triangle drawn in copper-gold ink on parchment, with each of its three vertices marked by a small icon: lower-left vertex an open coin purse (cost), lower-right vertex a stopwatch (speed), top vertex a magnifying lens over a flower (quality). At the geometric center of the triangle: a small hand-drawn loom, rendered in indigo. Three thin threads run from the loom's center out to each of the three vertices, connecting them. The triangle is *not* showing tradeoffs; it is showing reinforcement. Pen-and-ink with selective copper and indigo. Square composition.

## Part III — The How

### Chapter 11 — Cells

> A microscopic cross-section of a biological cell rendered in the style of a 1930s scientific illustration plate, but the cell's three internal organelles are *labeled* (in tiny ink legends) Loom, Weaver, Grimoire — represented by a small loom, a small spinning wheel, and a small leather-bound book respectively. The cell membrane is intact and the cell is set against a parchment background as if from a textbook. Pen-and-ink with selective watercolor wash — pale indigo cytoplasm, copper organelles. Square. The illustration is the book's clearest single-image explanation of what a Cell is.

### Chapter 12 — WRAG

> A scholar's writing desk in the style of a Vermeer interior, seen from a slight angle. On the desk: an open ancient book (the Grimoire), with a single hand drawing a delicate copper thread from the page. The thread leaves the page and rises to a small floating loom hovering above the desk, where the thread is being woven into a piece of fresh cloth (the response). The book stays open on the desk; only threads relevant to the moment leave. Warm afternoon light from a window. Pen-and-ink with rich watercolor — indigo book, parchment desk, copper thread, ivory cloth. Landscape.

### Chapter 13 — The Capability Bus

> A peer-to-peer network diagram drawn as a constellation, in the style of an old astronomical chart. A dozen small wooden cottages (the Cells) are scattered across the parchment night sky as constellation points. Between them, signed messages are shown as thin copper-gold lines arcing through the air, each line marked with a small wax-seal at its midpoint (the cryptographic signature). No central station. No hub. Each cottage glows softly. Pen-and-ink with watercolor wash. Deep indigo background, copper accents, ivory cottages. Landscape. The image argues: cooperation without center.

### Chapter 14 — The Compute Farm

> A small farming village at twilight, rendered as a pen-and-ink etching with watercolor wash. Each cottage is unmistakably also a small computational unit — through their windows we glimpse small loom-shaped hearths glowing with three different intensities (some dark/cold, some dim/warm, some bright/hot). A network of soft copper-gold light-threads runs along the village's lanes, connecting houses that are currently doing federated work. A few cottages are dark and silent (Cold tier, sleeping until needed). The village is alive with ordinary domestic activity overlaid with subtle computational cooperation. Earthy palette, copper accents, indigo dusk. Landscape.

### Chapter 15 — BYOK

> A merchant's strongroom in the style of a 17th-century Dutch interior. Center: a sturdy wooden chest with three brass keyholes. The user's hand (foreground, calloused, ungloved) is inserting a single brass key — *their own key* — into one of the locks. Behind the chest, faintly, the silhouette of a great trading house (the frontier provider) with its doors open and a clerk's desk visible inside; the clerk has the matching key on the other side. The user is the contractual principal, dealing directly with the trading house, not through an intermediary. Pen-and-ink with rich watercolor — brass keys, oak chest, indigo shadows. Square or landscape.

### Chapter 16 — The Architecture of Sovereignty

> A constitutional charter laid open on a wooden table, with five articles visible (corresponding to the five rights). Around the charter on the table: a quill, a wax seal stamp, a small wooden stamp-press. The charter's edges are ornate but the body is simply written. A single hand (calloused, working-class) rests at the bottom of the page beside the seal stamp, ready to seal what the user has read. Pen-and-ink with watercolor wash. Parchment, indigo ink, copper-red wax. Square or slightly landscape. The image argues: this is not a EULA; this is a charter the user signs and seals.

### Chapter 17 — Privacy by Design

> A defense-in-depth fortification plan in the style of a Vauban-era military engineer's drawing. Five concentric polygonal walls, each labeled with thin call-out marks (the four threat surfaces and the residual-risk perimeter). At the center: a small dwelling with a warm light visible through its window. Each ring has clearly different defensive features — outer wall (network), middle wall (peer federation), inner wall (BYOK boundary), inmost wall (hardware). Pen-and-ink in muted ochre and indigo on parchment. Landscape. The image is both a beautiful technical drawing and a substantive map of the chapter's threat model.

## Part IV — The Versus

### Chapter 18 — Cost Architectures Compared

> A 19th-century ledger book opened to a double-page spread. Left page: handwritten entries titled "Hyperscale" with growing columns of figures (subscription, margin, capacity provisioning), summing to a large total at the bottom. Right page: handwritten entries titled "Warp" with much shorter columns (electricity, hardware amortization, BYOK invocations) summing to a much smaller total. Both pages in the same hand. A single quill rests in the gutter between them. The image is an honest accounting laid open. Pen-and-ink, parchment, indigo ink. Landscape.

### Chapter 19 — Environmental Footprints Compared

> A diptych in the style of Currier and Ives, two scenes side by side, joined by a horizon line. Left scene: an industrial-scale data center on stripped land, cooling towers belching, river running dry, smokestacks against gray sky. Right scene: a quiet rural village with cottages glowing softly at dusk, a healthy stream, trees in autumn color, a clean sky. Both scenes are rendered in the same hand and palette so the comparison is apples-to-apples, not cartoon-to-cartoon. The visual contrast is the chapter's argument made plain. Pen-and-ink with watercolor wash. Landscape.

### Chapter 20 — Privacy Postures Compared

> Two data-flow diagrams drawn as river systems on parchment. Left: the user's query enters as a single small stream and is then split, branched, dammed, diverted, and pooled across many distinct watersheds (logs, training, safety review, third parties, legal process). Right: the user's query stays in a single small pond on the user's own property, with a tiny trickle going out only to a clearly-marked downstream consumer (BYOK provider) under the user's authorization. Pen-and-ink with watercolor — indigo water, ochre land, copper boundary lines. Landscape. The image renders a complex argument as immediately legible cartography.

### Chapter 21 — Capability and Quality Compared

> A pair of antique brass scales as in Chapter 9, but here the *line* of "good enough" is drawn across both pans as a horizontal thread, and the scales show two systems — frontier on left, Warp's cooperative capability on the right — both above the line, with the right pan's content (a Cell with attached corpus and BYOK access) visibly *taller* than the left pan because of its grounding context. The thread of "good enough" is moving downward (suggested by faint ghosted lines). Pen-and-ink with brass and copper accents. Square. Landscape works too.

### Chapter 22 — What Centralized AI Is Used For — and What Warp Refuses

> A heavy oak door, partially closed, with a hand-painted "REFUSED" placard nailed to it (the wording is illustrated, not legible — it's a placard, not English text in the image). Through the partially-open door, glimpses of what lies beyond: faintly, a military targeting display; faintly, a surveillance camera bank; faintly, a behavioral-targeting console. The door's hinge bears a small wax seal — the Foundation's mark. The composition: someone has shut this door deliberately. Pen-and-ink with deep indigo and rust shadow. Landscape. The image is the most politically charged in the book and should be rendered with restraint, not melodrama.

A copper-gold thread connects all the stations and exits the workshop through an open door, where it joins a much larger field of similar threads stretching toward an unseen horizon — the broader community. Pen-and-ink with watercolor — meadow greens, ivory stone, copper light. Landscape.

### Chapter 27 — The Path Forward

Along the path at various distances: small lit cottages, a meeting hall, a workshop — the institutions of the previous chapters. The path is unmistakably a beginning. Pen-and-ink with watercolor wash. Warm dawn palette — gold, ivory, soft indigo, with the rust and ochre of fresh-turned earth at the foreground. Landscape, wider than tall. The Lao Tzu epigraph made literal: the journey begins beneath the foot.

## The WARP Reference Architecture Diagram

This is the most important illustration in the book, and the one image generators are *least* equipped to render reliably, because precise text labels and connection lines matter. Two artifacts are needed:

1. **A structured spec** that can be drawn precisely in Excalidraw, Figma, Draw.io, OmniGraffle, or LaTeX/TikZ — recommended for the actual book.
2. **An image-generation prompt** that captures the spirit and could serve as an aesthetic frontispiece for the technical diagram, or as a placeholder until the precise version is rendered.

### 1. Structured Spec (for vector-tool rendering)

**Layout:** Landscape, three horizontal bands. The Cell occupies the central band. The user's environment is above; the broader federation and external providers are below.

**Top band — User Environment:**
- A single human figure icon (left) labeled *User / Owner*.
- An arrow from the User to a *Loom* box in the middle band.

**Middle band — A Single Cell** (the center of the diagram, a large rounded rectangle that contains everything below):

Inside the Cell rectangle, three sub-boxes arranged left-to-right:

- **Loom**
  - Sub-label: *Front-end / UI tier*
  - Purpose statement (small): "User-facing surface. Local web app, terminal, mobile, custom — interchangeable. The Cell's mouth and ears."
  - Connection: Loom ↔ Weaver (bidirectional arrow labeled *internal bus*)

- **Weaver**
  - Sub-label: *Inference & orchestration tier*
  - Purpose statement (small): "Decides what to do with each query: which model, which retrieval, which sensitivity policy, which provider. Policy enforcement boundary. Holds local model weights."
  - Connection: Weaver ↔ Loom; Weaver ↔ Grimoire; Weaver ↔ Capability Bus (outbound to peers); Weaver ↔ BYOK (outbound to providers).

- **Grimoire**
  - Sub-label: *Data tier*
  - Purpose statement (small): "User's persistent state: documents, conversation history, embeddings, audit logs, federation contracts, Cell identity keys. Encrypted at rest. The Cell's memory and records."

A vertical dotted line on the right edge of the Cell rectangle is labeled *Hardware boundary — data does not leave except by deliberate, owner-authorized action.*

**Bottom band — External World:**

Three groups of boxes, separated by space:

- **Capability Bus** (a horizontal arched arrow connecting the Weaver to peer Cells):
  - Multiple peer-Cell icons (small versions of the central Cell, drawn in miniature) connected to the central Weaver via signed-message arrows. Each arrow is annotated with a small wax-seal icon (the cryptographic signature).
  - Labels: *Peer Cell — Family*, *Peer Cell — Small Business*, *Peer Cell — Community*, *Peer Cell — Cooperative*. (Show 3–5 peer Cells; not all need labels.)
  - Group caption: *Capability Bus — pub/sub messaging fabric. Every invocation signed. No central server. Routes capabilities, not topics.*

- **BYOK / Frontier Providers** (right of the Capability Bus):
  - 2–3 provider boxes labeled *Frontier Provider A*, *Frontier Provider B*, *Frontier Provider C* (suggested examples in caption: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google).
  - Each connected to the Weaver via an arrow annotated with a small key icon.
  - Group caption: *BYOK — User's contractual relationship with provider. User holds the key. Minimum-necessary prompt assembled from Grimoire context. User is principal.*

- **Compute Farm tiers** (left of the Capability Bus, below the Cell):
  - Three small icons stacked vertically: a dark cottage (Cold), a dim cottage (Warm), a bright cottage (Hot).
  - Group caption: *Compute Farm — Cold (wake-on-demand), Warm (idle-ready), Hot (in-flight). Capacity tiers across federation peers.*

**Annotations / Margins:**

- Top right: small Foundation Pledge icon (a wax seal) with a tiny caption: *Foundation Pledge — refusals at the architectural level (Ch. 26).*
- Bottom right: small Cell Operator Covenant icon (an open hand) with a tiny caption: *Operator Covenant — community practice (Ch. 26).*
- Below the entire diagram, a single sweeping caption arc: *Warp Reference Architecture — User-Sovereign Synthetic Intelligence.*

**Color scheme** (matching the book's visual identity):
- Cell rectangle outline: deep charcoal
- Loom: warm ochre fill
- Weaver: deep indigo fill
- Grimoire: parchment ivory fill with a thin copper border
- Capability Bus arcs: copper-gold lines
- BYOK arrows: indigo with brass key icons
- Compute Farm cottages: warm earth tones graded from dark (cold) to bright (hot)

**Typography:** Use a single serif typeface throughout (Garamond, Crimson, EB Garamond) for consistency with the book's overall typesetting. Component labels in bold; purpose statements in regular weight, smaller; captions in italics, smaller still.

### 2. Image-Generation Prompt (aesthetic frontispiece for the technical diagram)

> A meticulous technical reference-architecture poster rendered in the style of a 19th-century engineering plate from a scientific encyclopaedia, on warm parchment background, in pen-and-ink with selective copper-gold and indigo highlights. Center: a large rounded-rectangle "Cell" containing three carefully labeled sub-components arranged left-to-right: a small wooden loom labeled "Loom (front-end)"; an indigo workbench with arrayed instruments labeled "Weaver (inference and orchestration)"; a leather-bound book on a stand labeled "Grimoire (data tier)". Around the Cell, four labeled groups: at the top, a stylized human figure labeled "User / Owner" with an arrow into the Loom; at the bottom-left, three small village cottages stacked vertically with brightness graded from dark to lit, labeled "Compute Farm — Cold / Warm / Hot"; at the bottom-center, a constellation of small peer cottages connected to the Weaver by arched copper-gold lines each marked with a tiny wax seal, labeled "Capability Bus — peer federation"; at the bottom-right, three stylized merchant-house facades labeled "Frontier Providers (BYOK)", connected to the Weaver by arrows each marked with a brass key. Margins contain a wax-seal icon labeled "Foundation Pledge" and an open-hand icon labeled "Operator Covenant". A scrolling caption banner along the bottom reads "WARP REFERENCE ARCHITECTURE — User-Sovereign Synthetic Intelligence". The aesthetic is craft-focused, considered, beautiful, legible — like a poster a small business would frame and hang in their server closet. Earthy palette of indigo, ochre, copper-gold, parchment, charcoal. Landscape orientation, 16:9, oversized for legibility.

### Recommended workflow

For the published book, draft the diagram in Excalidraw or Figma first, using the structured spec. Send it to a competent technical illustrator who can render it as a finished pen-and-ink plate matching the book's overall aesthetic. The image-generation prompt is useful for the proposal stage, the website, and as a visual reference for the illustrator — not as the final artifact for print. Diagrams with more than a handful of precise labels are a known weakness of current image-generation systems, and the WARP architecture diagram has too much labeled content to risk.

## Production Notes

- **One image per chapter is the recommended minimum.** Some chapters (1, 5, 11, 17, 22, 27) deserve a second illustrative image if the budget allows; their prompts can be adapted to alternate angles.
- **The cover prompt above offers two concepts.** Generate both, evaluate against the book's typesetting, and pick.
- **Keep the visual identity consistent.** Different chapters should feel like pages from the same hand. If you generate with multiple operators or models, prefer a single illustrator (human or AI) for the whole book to maintain coherence.
- **For images that contain in-image text (signage, scrolls, ledger entries):** the image models will mangle the text. Either accept that the text will be illustrative-only (legible in feel, not in letter), or generate the text-free version and add typography in post.
- **The Reference Architecture poster is worth printing as a standalone foldout** in physical editions of the book. It should also be downloadable from the Foundation's website at full resolution under the same CC BY 4.0 license as the manuscript.
- **Permissions and licensing.** Whichever illustrator (human or model) produces the final art, ensure the rights structure permits the same CC BY 4.0 redistribution the manuscript itself uses. If using AI-generated art, document the prompts used (this file is the start of that documentation) and the model and date of generation; this matters for both copyright clarity and historical record.
